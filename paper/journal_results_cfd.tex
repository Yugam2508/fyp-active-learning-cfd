\subsection{CFD dispersion case}
\label{sec:res-cfd}
\emph{The case.} Fig.~\ref{fig:cfd} shows the flow and concentration field
for the centre of the input box: the plume is lifted over the building,
part of it is trapped in a recirculating wake that extends several building
heights downstream, and the concentration at breathing height is nearly
uniform inside the wake and decays beyond it. Of the 2900 CFD runs of the
study (1550 for the designs and the reference, the rest added by the
adaptive designs), 80\% met the residual criteria within 4000
iterations (median 1322
iterations); for the others, mostly tall buildings whose steady wake keeps
the residuals oscillating, the receptor concentration varied by less than
0.16\% over the last 2000 iterations, so the iteration error is negligible.
Refining the mesh twice in each direction changed the receptor
concentration by 0.9--6.5\% (median 3.7\%) in six flow states spread over
the input box: a discretisation error that is part of the model as
defined, as it would be for any production CFD model. The reference gives
a mean of 434\,mg\,m$^{-3}$, an SD of 413\,mg\,m$^{-3}$, a 95th percentile
of 1252\,mg\,m$^{-3}$ and $P(C>\mathrm{IDLH})=0.673$, with standard
uncertainties from a bootstrap over the 800 reference runs of 2.3\%,
3.4\%, 3.3\% and 0.007; validation errors of the propagated quantities
below about 5\% are therefore not resolved.

\emph{Specification and selection.} The CFD case confirms the analytical
results (Table~\ref{tab:n20}, last row). Kernel specification did not
matter (ratio 0.96), but the log scale did: the log-variable GP had a
median nRMSE of 0.080 against 0.53 for the Mat\'ern GP (ratio 0.17, better
in all 30 seeds), and a quadratic trend on the log scale reduced it further
to 0.060 (ratio 0.76). Sparse PCE was 1.4 times worse than the GP on the raw
scale and 2.3 times on the log scale, and the CV-weighted committee was no
better than the GP with trend (ratio 1.08). The selection by LOO error
chose a log-scale GP in all 30 seeds and was within 0.1\% of the best
candidate in hindsight. With the log-variable GP, the 95th percentile was
within 6.9\% of the reference and $P(C>\mathrm{IDLH})$ within 0.009, against
18.5\% and 0.019 for the Mat\'ern GP and 27\% and 0.059 for the committee.

\emph{Adaptive designs.} Adding runs at the largest GP posterior SD did not
change the nRMSE (ratio 0.93), and AK-MCS did not improve
$P(C>\mathrm{IDLH})$ (ratio 0.94): the IDLH is exceeded in two thirds of the
cases, so the threshold lies in the bulk of the distribution rather than in
a tail, where a targeted design has most to gain. The adaptive committee
reduced the nRMSE relative to the passive one (ratio 0.84) but, as on the
plume model, its estimate of $P(C>\mathrm{IDLH})$ was off by a median of
0.11, against 0.06 for the passive committee and 0.009 for the log-variable
GP.

\emph{Surrogate contribution.} The coverage results carry over to the CFD
case (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}, last row; diamonds in
Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). Pointwise, the GP intervals covered 69--92\% of
test points and the jackknife+ intervals 94.8--99.1\%. Treating the
surrogate as exact gave GUM~S1 coverage intervals with an achieved coverage
of 90.8--94.7\%; adding the LOO error gave 93.8--95.7\%. The GP posterior
was most misleading for the 95th percentile: with the raw-scale GPs
(Mat\'ern, with or without trend) its 95\% interval contained the true
value in only 10\% of seeds, against 87--93\% for the jackknife; with the
log-variable GP the figures were 80\% and 97\%.
