\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
For GUM~S1 evaluations in which the measurement model can be run only a few
tens of times, the way a surrogate is specified matters more than the
strategy around it. A GP should estimate its amplitude and one length scale
per input and work on the log scale when the output is positive; a failure
of GP surrogates reported in an earlier analysis was an artefact of one
hyperparameter held fixed. A quadratic trend helps when the response has
one but can overfit when there are many inputs, so it belongs among the
candidates rather than in every model. Among such candidates and a sparse
PCE, the one with the smallest leave-one-out error
was as good as the best in hindsight on all twelve models, including a CFD
simulation of a release behind a building. Committees of surrogates and
variance-driven adaptive designs gave no consistent gain at 20 runs,
whereas a design targeted at the threshold improved exceedance
probabilities when the threshold lay in a tail of the distribution. Finally, the surrogate's own error must be part of the
uncertainty statement, and it should be estimated from leave-one-out
residuals rather than from the GP's predictive variance: the former
restored the nominal coverage of the GUM~S1 coverage interval and gave
valid intervals for reported quantities, the latter was overconfident
precisely where the surrogate was poor.
