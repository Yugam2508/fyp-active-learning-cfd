\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Uncertainty quantification (UQ) for high-fidelity computational fluid
dynamics (CFD) simulations of hazardous gas dispersion is constrained by a
severe budget mismatch: each run costs hours, while classical Monte Carlo
needs thousands of evaluations. We study surrogate-based UQ at a budget of
$N{=}20$ evaluations and make three contributions. \textbf{First}, we
characterise when the standard tool fails: Gaussian Process (GP) \emph{prior
reversion} yields near-constant, physically meaningless predictions only when
a large input domain \emph{and} a high output dynamic range coincide. A
domain-size ablation locates a sharp crossover, and a Schwefel control
($\rho{=}1.09$) shows that domain size alone is insufficient. \textbf{Second},
through paired $30$-seed ablations we dissect a heterogeneous-committee active
learning framework built on a spectral acquisition metric (UEigV). The
evidence contradicts the natural reading of such frameworks: plain ensemble
variance acquisition is equal-or-better than the spectral metric ($30/30$ vs
$27/30$ successes, Wilcoxon $p{=}0.047$); removing the degree-2 polynomial
chaos member collapses performance ($0/30$); and the choice of
\emph{aggregation rule} is decisive---uniform averaging over a committee that
contains the correct model fails in $30/30$ trials (median RMSE $1{,}763$),
hard rejection recovers accuracy only by collapsing to a single model and
silently degrading ${\approx}31\%$ of queries to random sampling, while
cross-validation inverse-error weighting attains both accuracy and usable
epistemic bands. \textbf{Third}, we complete the pipeline with a forward-UQ
stage (probability box, bounded exceedance probabilities, a variance
decomposition usable as a stopping diagnostic, and Sobol indices with
epistemic bars) that recovers ground-truth Monte Carlo to within ${\approx}1\%$.
All results are on a single synthetic benchmark whose dominant term lies in
one committee member's hypothesis class; we state this limitation up front
and release every per-trial record, including the negative results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The transition toward low-carbon energy has renewed interest in ammonia as a
hydrogen carrier, but ammonia is toxic and accidental releases form dense,
ground-hugging clouds whose spatial concentration must be predicted for
quantitative risk assessment~\cite{tan2017}. CFD is the industry standard for
such dispersion modelling, but a single high-resolution run can take hours to
days on HPC infrastructure. This is a fundamental barrier to UQ: Monte Carlo
(MC) converges as $N^{-1/2}$~\cite{jcgm2008}, so reducing error tenfold needs
$100\times$ more runs, while the feasible CFD budget is often fewer than
twenty evaluations.

Surrogate models---cheap emulators trained on a handful of high-fidelity
evaluations---are the standard remedy~\cite{wang2022,heidenreich2017}, with
GP regression the workhorse for computer experiments~\cite{sacks1989,rw2006}.
A surrogate that is overconfident in data-sparse regions, however,
contributes model-form uncertainty invisible to its own variance
estimate~\cite{huellermeier2021}. Committee-based active learning, which
queries where structurally different surrogates disagree, is a natural
response, and spectral disagreement measures from language-model
UQ~\cite{lin2024} are an appealing way to score that disagreement.

This paper is deliberately an \emph{ablation} paper. We began from such a
framework---heterogeneous committee, spectral UEigV acquisition, and a
consistency-based model manager---and asked which ingredient is responsible
for its performance. The answers were not the ones we expected, and we report
them as found. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item A falsifiable two-condition criterion (large domain $\times$ high
dynamic range) for when passive GP surrogates fail catastrophically
(Sec.~\ref{sec:failure}).
\item Paired ablations showing that, in this regime, (a) the spectral metric
confers no advantage over plain ensemble variance, and (b) success depends on
the committee containing an \emph{anchor} model whose hypothesis class matches
the response's dominant structure (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).
\item A characterisation of committee \emph{aggregation}: uniform averaging is
catastrophic in the prior-reversion regime, hard rejection is a degenerate
baseline, and cross-validation inverse-error weighting---the weighted-average
surrogate of~\cite{goel2007,viana2009}, which we do not claim as new---is
necessary for both accuracy and epistemic expressiveness
(Sec.~\ref{sec:aggregation}).
\item A forward-UQ stage that turns the trained committee into a Monte Carlo
engine with epistemic bars, validated against ground-truth MC
(Sec.~\ref{sec:uq}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Failure Characterisation}
\label{sec:failure}
For a GP with prior mean $\mu$ and kernel $k$, the posterior predictive mean
at a test point $\mathbf{x}^\ast$ is
\begin{equation}
\hat f(\mathbf{x}^\ast)=\mu+\mathbf{k}_\ast^\top
\!\left(\mathbf{K}+\sigma_\varepsilon^2\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\!(\mathbf{y}-\mu\mathbf{1}).
\label{eq:gp}
\end{equation}
As the minimum distance between $\mathbf{x}^\ast$ and the training set grows
relative to the length scale $\ell$, the RBF covariance
$k(\mathbf{x}^\ast,\mathbf{x}_i)\!=\!\sigma_f^2\exp(-\lVert\mathbf{x}^\ast-\mathbf{x}_i\rVert^2/2\ell^2)\!\to\!0$,
so $\hat f\to\mu$ regardless of the data. On the physics benchmark
$f_{\text{phys}}(\mathbf{x})=\sin(2x_1)\cos(2x_2)+e^{-x_3^2}+0.2\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2$
over $[-200,200]^3$, with $20$ Latin-Hypercube (LHS) samples the expected
nearest-neighbour distance is $\mathcal{O}(100)$ per dimension; the marginal
likelihood cannot identify a length scale long enough to bridge the gaps, and
the GP reverts to its standardised prior mean across most of the domain.

\paragraph{Domain-size ablation.} Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} evaluates the
benchmark at half-widths $h\in\{2,10,50,100,200\}$ with all other settings
fixed. A sharp crossover appears between $h{=}2$ ($\rho{=}1.06$,
indistinguishable) and $h{=}10$ ($\rho{=}0.384$), where
$\rho=\text{RMSE}_{\text{AL}}/\text{RMSE}_{\text{LHS+GP}}$.

\paragraph{The Schwefel control.} The Schwefel function on $[-500,500]^3$ has
a \emph{larger} domain than the physics benchmark, yet active learning does
not win ($\rho{=}1.09$). Its output varies only between $\pm 418d$ with the
minimum near zero, so the GP prior mean is a reasonable approximation even in
data-sparse regions. Catastrophic failure therefore requires large domain size
\emph{and} high output dynamic range. This two-condition criterion is the
paper's central positive finding. As Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation} shows, it is
necessary but not sufficient for a committee to help.

\section{Framework Under Study}
\label{sec:framework}

\paragraph{Heterogeneous committee.} A committee of $K{=}9$ surrogates
spans complementary inductive biases: two GPs (RBF and Mat\'ern-$3/2$), two
Polynomial Chaos Expansions (PCE, degrees $2$ and $3$, Bayesian Ridge),
two Random Forests ($50$/$100$ trees), an RBF Support Vector Regressor, and
two DNNs ($2{\times}64$, $2{\times}128$). Structurally different models
extrapolate differently in data-sparse regions---a GP reverts to its mean, a
PCE follows the global polynomial, an RF returns leaf means---so their
disagreement is a model-form uncertainty signal~\cite{lakshmi2017}.

\paragraph{UEigV acquisition.} At a candidate $\mathbf{x}$ the $K$ predictions
form nodes of a fully connected graph with RBF edge weights
$A_{ij}=\exp(-(\hat y_i-\hat y_j)^2/2\sigma^2)$. With normalised Laplacian
$\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$ and
eigenvalues $\lambda_k\in[0,2]$, the acquisition score is
\begin{equation}
\text{UEigV}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\max\!\left(0,\,1-\lambda_k\right),
\label{eq:ueigv}
\end{equation}
a continuous relaxation of the number of agreement clusters~\cite{lin2024}.
The next query maximises UEigV over a proximity-filtered random candidate
pool. The natural comparator is plain query-by-committee \emph{variance},
$\operatorname{Var}_k[\hat y_k(\mathbf{x})]$.

\paragraph{Model management.} Two managers are studied. The \emph{hard}
Largest Consistent Subset (LCS) manager scores each model by its reduced
chi-square deviation from the ensemble mean and permanently rejects the worst
if it exceeds the $\alpha$-quantile threshold ($\alpha{=}0.99$); predictions
are the uniform mean of survivors. The \emph{soft} manager weights rather
than removes, using $k$-fold cross-validation error,
\begin{equation}
w_k\;\propto\;\frac{1}{\text{CV-MSE}_k\,(1+\pi_k)},
\label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
with an optional physics penalty $\pi_k\ge0$ for violations of known output
bounds (e.g.\ concentration non-negativity)~\cite{karniadakis2021}. Models
below a weight floor are transiently muted from the acquisition graph but
keep retraining and may recover; at least two models always remain active.
Equation~\eqref{eq:soft} is the weighted-average surrogate
of~\cite{goel2007,viana2009}; we use it, we do not propose it.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Committee active learning with model management}
\label{alg:al}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require domain $\mathcal{X}$, budget $N_{\max}$, $N_0{=}5$, manager $\mathcal{M}$
\State $\mathcal{D}_0\gets\text{LHS}(N_0,\mathcal{X})$; train committee on $\mathcal{D}_0$
\For{$t=1,\dots,N_{\max}-N_0$}
  \State sample candidate pool $\mathcal{X}_c$ (proximity-filtered)
  \State $\mathbf{x}^\ast\gets\arg\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}_c}a(\mathbf{x})$ \Comment{UEigV or variance}
  \State $y^\ast\gets f(\mathbf{x}^\ast)$;\;\; $\mathcal{D}_t\gets\mathcal{D}_{t-1}\cup\{(\mathbf{x}^\ast,y^\ast)\}$
  \State retrain all active models on $\mathcal{D}_t$; apply $\mathcal{M}$
\EndFor
\State \Return committee, weights, $\mathcal{D}_{N_{\max}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{What Drives Performance: Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Every arm uses the physics benchmark on
$[-200,200]^3$, a budget of $N{=}20$ ($5$ LHS $+$ $15$ queries) and $30$
seeds ($1000$--$1029$), \emph{paired}: the same seed gives every arm the same
initial design. RMSE is measured on one fixed $5{,}000$-point test set.
Paired arms are compared by a Wilcoxon signed-rank test on log-RMSE. A trial
is a \emph{success} if RMSE $<200$, an order of magnitude below the
${\approx}2{,}000$ level of a prior-reverted GP; the two populations are
separated by more than two orders of magnitude, so the threshold is not
sensitive.

\label{tab:ablation}
\footnotesize\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}

\toprule

\paragraph{The spectral metric is not the mechanism.} Replacing UEigV with
plain ensemble variance, with everything else unchanged, succeeds in all
$30$ trials against $27$ for UEigV, and has lower paired RMSE in $18/30$
seeds ($p{=}0.047$, favouring variance). On this benchmark the spectral
metric adds nothing over query-by-committee variance, and we withdraw the
claim that it does.

\paragraph{The anchor model is the mechanism.} Removing the two PCE members
from the committee makes the framework fail in every trial (median RMSE
$2{,}387$); the surviving model is then a DNN or GP that prior-reverts. The
standard adaptive baseline---a single GP querying its posterior standard
deviation---fails identically ($0/30$), which confirms that the regime of
Sec.~\ref{sec:failure} defeats variance-driven sampling by a lone GP.
Instrumenting the full framework shows why it succeeds: hard LCS
sequentially rejects the misspecified majority and ends with PCE-d2 in
$26/30$ trials (PCE-d3 and GP-RBF in two each). PCE-d2 is the exact
hypothesis class of the benchmark's dominant $0.2\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2$
term, which carries $>99.9\%$ of the output variance, and no query lands in the
oscillatory interior ($r<30$). The final RMSE of ${\approx}0.7$ is the RMS of
the unresolved $\sin\cdot\cos$ term. The win is therefore
consistency-based \emph{model selection} funnelling onto an anchor model, not
an interior discovery event driven by spectral disagreement.

\section{Aggregation Is the Decisive Choice}
\label{sec:aggregation}

\paragraph{Hard LCS is a degenerate baseline.} Hard LCS collapses to a
single surviving model in $30/30$ trials, at median AL iteration $10$ of $15$.
With fewer than two models, both UEigV and variance are constant across the
candidate pool and $\arg\max$ returns an arbitrary pool member: ${\approx}31\%$
of hard LCS's query budget is silently \emph{uniform random sampling}. The
soft manager's two-model floor prevents this. A naive soft-vs-hard comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, last row: soft better in $21/30$ seeds, $p{=}0.004$)
therefore measures soft CV weighting against a crippled baseline.

\paragraph{Decomposition.} Two further experiments isolate the cause.
\emph{Predictor swap:} predicting from hard LCS's own final dataset with the
CV-weighted all-nine ensemble instead of the survivor mean changes nothing
($p{=}0.237$; $25/30$ pairs within $1\%$), because both predictors reduce to
PCE-d2. \emph{Fair baseline:} forcing hard LCS to keep at least three models
(the only change) exposes the aggregation rule (Table~\ref{tab:aggr}).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} With three survivors (typically GP-RBF, PCE-d2
and PCE-d3), a \emph{uniform} mean fails in every trial even though the
correct model is present: the prior-reverted GP's error of order $10^3$
dominates the average. The same committee with CV inverse-error weights
succeeds in every trial. Reversibility of rejection contributes nothing
measurable; the causal ingredient is the aggregation rule. The surviving
claim is therefore: \emph{in the GP prior-reversion regime, committee
aggregation must be error-weighted. Uniform averaging fails catastrophically;
hard rejection to a single model recovers accuracy only by discarding all
epistemic information and degrading the acquisition; CV inverse-error
weighting attains both accuracy and usable epistemic bands.} This is a
characterisation of when and why an existing method~\cite{goel2007,viana2009}
is necessary, not a new method.

\paragraph{Weighting schemes that fail.} Two plausible alternatives to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft} fail and are instructive. (i) Boltzmann weights on the
training-point chi-square score ($w_k\!\propto\!e^{-s_k/2T}$) leave
catastrophic extrapolators near-uniformly weighted (RMSE ${\approx}2{,}000$),
because every surrogate interpolates the $20$ training points and the score
cannot separate them. (ii) Consensus-deviation probe scores penalise the
single \emph{correct} dissenter when the majority prior-reverts together.
CV error sidesteps both by scoring each model against held-out data, never
against the committee, which separates members by orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{Cross-benchmark context.} Table~\ref{tab:cross} reports the
original framework (UEigV, hard LCS) against LHS+GP on nine problems. Only the
physics benchmark---the one problem satisfying both failure
conditions---favours active learning ($\rho{=}0.044$ on mean RMSE, which the
passive GP's catastrophic trials inflate); on seven problems a
well-specified GP ties or wins by $9$--$17\%$. In light of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}, this table delineates the regime but does not
attribute the physics-benchmark gain to the spectral metric.

\section{Forward Uncertainty Quantification}
\label{sec:uq}
The trained committee is a Monte Carlo engine: a $50{,}000$-point study costs
seconds on the surrogate versus ${\sim}50{,}000$ CFD-hours on the solver. We
propagate the input distribution through all surrogates and report four
quantities, each with epistemic bars from the weighted committee, validated
against a $200{,}000$-point ground-truth MC on the synthetic benchmark.

\paragraph{Output PDF and probability box.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pbox} shows the CDF probability box: at each level $y$ the
committee CDFs span $[F_{\text{low}}(y),F_{\text{up}}(y)]$, a p-box in the
sense of~\cite{ferson1996}. Exceedance probabilities follow as bounded
intervals. For a $95$th-percentile safety threshold, $P(Y>y_c)$ is returned as
an interval rather than a single unjustified point estimate.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_uq_pbox.pdf}
\caption{CDF probability box and bounded exceedance probability (soft
manager, representative seed). The band is the committee envelope, the solid
line the weighted-mixture CDF, the dashed line the ground-truth CDF.}
\label{fig:pbox}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Variance decomposition as a stopping diagnostic.}
By the law of total variance over the input distribution,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}[Y]=\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf{x}}\!\big[\mu_w(\mathbf{x})\big]}_{\text{input-driven}}
+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\!\big[\sigma^2_{\text{epi}}(\mathbf{x})\big]}_{\text{epistemic}}
\;(+\,\sigma^2_\varepsilon).
\label{eq:lotv}
\end{equation}
The epistemic fraction (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}) is a convergence diagnostic:
while it dominates, the next evaluation should buy another simulation; once it
is small, the UQ estimate is committee-converged. In the representative run it
decays from $0.76$ to $\approx 0$ over the budget. Because the bands are
committee envelopes, ``converged'' means the \emph{committee} agrees, not
that the truth has been reached.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_uq_decomp.pdf}
\caption{Law-of-total-variance decomposition versus sample count. Left:
input-driven and epistemic variance. Right: the epistemic fraction, a
committee-convergence diagnostic.}
\label{fig:decomp}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Sensitivity with epistemic bars.}
First-order and total Sobol indices are estimated by the Saltelli/Jansen
pick-and-freeze scheme~\cite{saltelli2010,jansen1999} on the cheap ensemble,
with per-model indices furnishing error bars on the sensitivity \emph{ranking}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sobol}). PCE-based analytic indices~\cite{sudret2008} are the
natural alternative when a PCE dominates the weights.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_uq_sobol.pdf}
\caption{Sobol first-order and total indices of the weighted ensemble. Error
bars are committee spread (epistemic); black ticks are ground-truth indices.}
\label{fig:sobol}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Validation over $30$ seeds.} Central estimates are accurate
whenever the run succeeds: the median relative error of the predicted output
median is $0.24\%$ for both managers, and a representative run matches truth
to within $1\%$ on mean, standard deviation and all reported quantiles. For
the soft manager, the mean band covers the true output mean in $97\%$ of
trials, the p-box brackets on average $68\%$ of the true CDF, and the $95$th
percentile exceedance interval covers the truth in $60\%$ of trials. These are
committee envelopes, not calibrated intervals, and they under-cover.
Conformal calibration~\cite{angelopoulos2023} is the principled fix. We do not
present the soft-versus-hard coverage contrast (Fig.~\ref{fig:campaign}) as
a result: a one-model ``ensemble'' has zero-width bands by construction, so
hard LCS's $0\%$ coverage is tautological. The figure illustrates what
collapsing to $K{=}1$ costs a UQ practitioner.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\paragraph{Single benchmark, favourable anchor.} Every ablation result is on
one synthetic benchmark whose dominant term coincides exactly with PCE-d2's
hypothesis class, and the framework fails outright without that member.
Nothing in Secs.~\ref{sec:ablation}--\ref{sec:aggregation} is claimed to
generalise until it is repeated on benchmarks with no anchor model in the
committee; this is the immediate next step. The two-condition failure
criterion (Sec.~\ref{sec:failure}) is supported across nine benchmarks and a
domain sweep, and is the most general result here.

\paragraph{Prior art.} CV inverse-error weighting is the weighted-average
surrogate of Goel et al.~\cite{goel2007} and Viana et al.~\cite{viana2009}.
Stacking~\cite{yao2018} and Bayesian model averaging~\cite{hoeting1999} are
related. Our contribution is to show when this aggregation is necessary and
what fails without it, not the rule itself.

\paragraph{Other limitations.} The benchmark is a smooth synthetic
construction; real CFD responses include sharp gradients and recirculation, so
validation on a live solver is required before safety-critical use.
Cross-validation at $N\!\approx\!20$ is itself noisy, so the weights should be
read as order-of-magnitude statements, which is all inverse-error weighting
needs. Soft weighting adds $k\times K$ refits per update (about $2\times$
the base loop), which is negligible next to one CFD run. The aggregation table
mixes DNN backends (scikit-learn MLP and PyTorch, architecturally identical);
the mechanism is arithmetic rather than stochastic, but a single-backend rerun
is planned. DNN surrogates are two-hidden-layer ReLU networks (Adam,
$800$ epochs, standardised I/O).

\section{Conclusion}
Passive GP surrogates fail catastrophically when the input domain is large
enough to cause prior reversion \emph{and} the response has enough dynamic
range to make the prior mean wrong. In that regime, a heterogeneous committee
can recover accuracy at the same $20$-evaluation budget, but paired ablations
show that the reason is not spectral acquisition, which plain variance matches
or beats. Two things are responsible: a committee member whose hypothesis
class matches the dominant response structure, and an error-weighted
aggregation rule that lets that member dominate without discarding the rest.
Uniform averaging fails even with the right model present. Hard rejection
succeeds only by collapsing to one model and randomising a third of its
queries. With CV inverse-error weighting, the committee also supports a
forward-UQ stage (probability box, bounded exceedance, a variance-decomposition
stopping diagnostic and Sobol indices with epistemic bars) that recovers
ground-truth MC to about $1\%$. Establishing whether these findings survive
without an anchor model is the open question on which any broader claim
depends.
